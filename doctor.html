What the check covers

  1. Structure. Missing = signs, directives outside any section, misspelled section headers, unknown directives (with a did-you-mean when it's a typo like Restar=always).
  2. Execution. Relative ExecStart paths (the 203/EXEC killer), shell syntax that systemd won't interpret, multiple ExecStart lines without Type=oneshot, Type=forking without a PIDFile.
  3. Values. Invalid Restart=, Type=, KillMode=, StandardOutput= — including the classic jurnal typo.
  4. Schedules. OnCalendar= is parsed with the same engine as our OnCalendar editor, and "every 5 min" in plain English gets translated to *:0/5.

Quick questions

Does this replace systemd-analyze verify?

No, and nothing browser-based can. systemd-analyze verify resolves dependencies and paths against your actual system. This check catches the authoring mistakes — the ones that cause most first failures — instantly, from any machine, without SSH. Use both.

My unit contains hostnames and paths. Are they safe here?

Yes. The file is parsed by JavaScript in your tab and never sent anywhere — open your browser's network tab and watch: nothing leaves.

Which unit types are understood?

.service, .timer, .socket, .mount, .path and .target sections, with the deepest rules on service and timer files — the two types people hand-write most.
