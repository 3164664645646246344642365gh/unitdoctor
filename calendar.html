    Reading an OnCalendar spec
    
    
      
      
    1. Three (or two) parts. Weekday Year-Month-Day Hour:Minute:Second.
      The weekday comes first — that's the part cron habits get wrong. Leave any part out
      and it means "any".
      2. 
      
    2. Ranges and steps. Mon..Fri is a range,
      09,12,18 is a list, *:0/15 is "starting at :00, every 15 minutes".
      Two dots for ranges — not the cron hyphen (both are accepted, but two dots are canonical).
      3. 
      
    3. Presets. minutely hourly daily monthly weekly yearly (and
      quarterly, semiannually) expand to the full form — the canonical
      output shows exactly what each one means.
      4. 
      
    4. Verify on the box. systemd-analyze calendar 'EXPRESSION'
      is the final authority and prints the same kind of list you see here.
      5. 
    
    
  
    

  
    
    
    Quick questions
    
    
    
      Which timezone does the preview use?
      
    Your browser's. systemd uses the server's local timezone unless you add
      Timezone= to the [Timer] section or write a UTC suffix on the expression —
      if your server runs UTC and you don't, that's the classic 8-hour surprise.
    
    
    
    
    
      Why does the canonical form differ from what I typed?
      
    The parser normalizes: presets expand, "9:0" becomes "09:00", cron-style
      1-5 ranges become 1..5. Same meaning, canonical spelling — paste
      the canonical form into your .timer file.
    
    
    
    
    
      Is this crontab.guru?
      
    It's the OnCalendar counterpart. crontab.guru is excellent for cron's 5 fields; systemd
      timers have a different grammar, and this editor speaks it natively — presets, ranges,
      seconds and all.
